# Performance

Two-layer INT8 network, 16-32-10, requantization shift 8.
One inference is **832 multiply-accumulates** (16x32 + 32x10, one per weight).

Generated by `python/perf_report.py` (`make perf`), which builds and runs
`tb/nn_accelerator_tb.sv` at each width and reads the testbench's own cycle
accounting. The testbench checks that accounting against the documented
formula on every run, so these are measurements rather than predictions.

> These are simulated cycle counts. No clock frequency is implied, and there
> are no FPGA or ASIC figures here — no frequency, power, LUT, DSP or
> wall-clock speedup — because this design has only ever been simulated.

## Measured

| MACs | Weight load | Latency | Interval, waiting for `done` | Interval, X always offered | Slower layer alone |
|-----:|------------:|--------:|-----------------------------:|---------------------------:|-------------------:|
| 1 | 834 | 892 | 893 | **860** | 562 |
| 4 | 834 | 284 | 285 | **252** | 178 |
| 8 | 834 | 188 | 189 | **156** | 114 |
| 16 | 834 | 124 | 125 | **92** | 82 |
| 32 | 834 | 108 | 109 | **76** | 76 |

*Weight load* is paid once and reused by every inference after it. *Latency*
is the busy window of one inference. The two *interval* columns are the
spacing between inferences: a host that waits for `done` gets the first, one
that keeps the next X offered gets the second, because layer 1 is a separate
FSM and starts while layer 2 is still computing. It cannot beat the slower
layer's own period, shown in the last column.

## Derived

| MACs | MAC ops / inference | Achieved ops/cycle | Peak ops/cycle | Utilization | Speedup vs 1 MAC |
|-----:|--------------------:|-------------------:|---------------:|------------:|-----------------:|
| 1 | 832 | 0.97 | 1 | 96.7% | 1.00x |
| 4 | 832 | 3.30 | 4 | 82.5% | 3.41x |
| 8 | 832 | 5.33 | 8 | 66.7% | 5.51x |
| 16 | 832 | 9.04 | 16 | 56.5% | 9.35x |
| 32 | 832 | 10.95 | 32 | 34.2% | 11.32x |

One MAC operation is one multiply-accumulate, not two flops. Achieved
ops/cycle is the MAC operations per inference divided by the streamed
interval. Peak is `NUM_MACS`, the active array's width: each layer
instantiates its own array, so the network holds `2*NUM_MACS` multipliers,
but only one layer computes at a time, which is why utilization is quoted
against the active array.

## What the numbers say

Going from 1 to 32 MACs shortens the interval
11.32x, from 860 cycles to 76. Utilization falls from 96.7% to 34.2% over the same
range: the input load, the activation handoff and the output beats do not
shrink with `NUM_MACS`, and the output layer has only
10 columns, so above 10 MACs some of its multipliers have nothing to do.

The weight load is a flat 834 cycles at every width: it is one byte per cycle
through the same port, and more multipliers do not widen it. Because weights
stay resident, a run of *n* inferences costs
834 + 860*n* cycles at one MAC and
834 + 76*n* at 32.
